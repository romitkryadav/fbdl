How to download Facebook photos

Three easy steps to save public Facebook photos to your device.

Step 1: Copy the Facebook link

Open Facebook, find the public post or album with photos, tap Share, and copy the URL.

Step 2: Paste it here

Paste the copied URL into the image downloader input box above and press the Download button.

Step 3: Save the photos

Click the Download button on each image once processing is complete, and the photo saves directly to your device.

What You Can Pull From a Link

This tool works with photos that anyone can already see without logging into Facebook: photos inside public posts, photos on public Pages, and public albums. If a link points to a profile or cover picture specifically, it'll work too — but if that's the only thing you're after, the Facebook DP Downloader is built around just that job and gets you there faster.

When a Link Has More Than One Photo

Posts and albums often hold more than one image. When your link points to one of these, the tool shows every photo it can find from that link as a preview grid, and you choose which ones to save — not an automatic bulk download of the whole album, but a pick-what-you-want result. What shows up depends on the specific link and what's actually public at that address; a link to an album won't surface photos the album owner restricted from public view.

Why Your Download Might Look Smaller Than the Post

The file you get is the version Facebook is currently serving at that link — not a screenshot, and not an upscaled or enhanced copy. Facebook compresses and resizes photos for its own performance reasons, so what you download can be smaller or more compressed than what was originally uploaded. There's no way for any downloader, this one included, to add back detail Facebook already removed. If you're after the best version available, open the photo to its own full-size view on Facebook before copying the link — that URL points to a larger version than a thumbnail in a feed.

Public Photos Only

"Public" means anyone can open it without being logged in or friends with the poster. If a photo is restricted to friends, shared in a closed or secret group, or limited to a specific audience, this tool has no way to reach it — it sees exactly what a logged-out visitor sees, nothing more. That's not a setting you can toggle around; it's how the tool works by design.

Getting It Onto Your Phone or Computer

The steps are the same wherever you're doing this: copy the post or photo link from Facebook, paste it above, wait for the preview, then download. On a computer, saved photos land in your browser's downloads folder. On a phone, check your Photos or Gallery app first, then your Downloads or Files app if you don't see it there — where it lands depends on your browser and device settings, not on this tool.

If Nothing Downloads

A few things typically cause this:

  • The photo or post is private — friends-only, group-restricted, or limited to a specific audience.
  • The post was deleted or the link is outdated.
  • The link points to a profile or feed generally, not a specific photo or album — try opening the exact photo first, then copying that page's link.
  • Facebook changed how it serves that content. This happens occasionally; if a link that used to work suddenly doesn't, that's usually why.

Using a Downloaded Photo

Saving a photo doesn't transfer ownership of it to you. The person who posted it — or whoever originally took or holds rights to it — typically still owns it, public or not. Keeping a copy for yourself is different from reposting it, editing it, or using it commercially; those uses depend on the photo, the photographer, and where you are, and this tool doesn't make that determination for you.
